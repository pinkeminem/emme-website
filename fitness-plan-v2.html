Phase 1 Focus

This phase is about learning to feel your glutes fire. Most people are "quad dominant" — they squat and feel it in their quads, not their glutes. Slow down the reps (3 sec down, 1 sec squeeze at top) until you feel the right muscle working.

Diet: don't stress calories yet. Start the FODMAP transition — swap out garlic, onion, wheat, and high-lactose dairy. Use garlic-infused oil (FODMAP safe) and the green tops of scallions instead. This week is about building the habit, not perfection.

Phase 2 Focus

Hip thrusts are the most important new movement. If you don't have a gym: use a couch or low bench, place a heavy backpack across your hips. Progress the weight every 1–2 weeks — even small increases matter.

Now start tracking calories loosely. Target 1,800 cal/day, 130–145g protein. FODMAP-safe protein stars: eggs, chicken, canned tuna/salmon, lactose-free Greek yogurt, firm tofu, tempeh (in small portions).

Running intervals will start cutting your 5K time toward 27–28 min by end of this phase.

Phase 3 Focus

Tighten calories to 1,600–1,750/day. Keep protein at 140–155g. This is the hardest phase nutritionally — you'll feel the deficit. That's expected. Fuel your training days slightly more (add 100–150 cal on Mon/Thu).

By now your glutes and thighs should be noticeably fuller and firmer. The shape change outpaces the scale — don't get fixated on the number if you're seeing visual change and strength gains.

If you're on HRT: this phase coincides with where training + hormones compound together most visibly. Estrogen preferentially deposits fat in hips and thighs, and the muscle you've built here gives that fat a rounder, fuller shape to fill.

Core Principles what actually moves the needle
01
Hip Thrusts Are Non-Negotiable
This is the movement most directly linked to glute growth. Every week, every phase. Increase the load every 1–2 weeks. Even 2.5 lbs more counts as progressive overload.
02
Train the Glute Med for Width
Clamshells, lateral band walks, and abduction work target the gluteus medius — the muscle that creates hip width. Most programs skip it. Don't. It's the difference between a flat vs. rounded silhouette.
03
Stretch Your Hip Flexors
Tight hip flexors inhibit glute activation — your glutes literally can't fire properly. Low lunge, pigeon, couch stretch daily. Your yoga practice is actually an asset here — use it.
04
Protein Every Meal, FODMAP Safe
140g/day minimum. Eggs, chicken, salmon, lactose-free Greek yogurt, firm tofu. Without protein, you lose the muscle you're building. With it, you build shape while cutting fat.
05
Pre-Plan Your Munchies
You smoke daily — plan for it. Portion your late snack before you smoke. Rice cakes + peanut butter, LF cottage cheese, protein bar (check for FODMAP-safe ingredients). Don't freestyle the kitchen.
06
The Scale Lies Mid-Program
Muscle weighs more than fat. In weeks 4–8 you may gain 1–2 lbs while visually transforming. Trust measurements (hips, thighs) and how clothes fit over scale weight. Body recomp doesn't always show on the scale first.
A note on HRT + training
If you're on estrogen, training and hormones work together, not separately. Estrogen gradually redistributes fat toward hips, thighs, and glutes over months to years. Resistance training accelerates this by building the underlying muscle that gives that redistribution its shape. The glute and hip work in this program is directly synergistic with feminizing HRT. You're building the structure that the hormones will fill in.
